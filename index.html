Patch notes for September 16. Enemies stopped shuffling a deck and started thinking; monster swarms, a player market, and a new hub join the world.

Enemy AI

Enemies got a brain

The old system gave every enemy a tiny 6-card deck it drew from — meaning it saw its whole kit by turn one anyway. That's gone, replaced by a kit of moves on individual cooldowns, plus real targeting logic.

No more "random" draw — enemies now run a readable rotation
Every enemy's whole kit is always available; each move is gated by its own cooldown instead of a shared deck. Expect the filler attack most turns, the secondary every couple of turns, and the signature move landing on a schedule instead of whenever it happened to be drawn.
Fixed the back-and-forth pacing bug
When two players stood near an enemy, it used to flip-flop which one it walked toward every turn. Enemies now commit to a target and only reconsider once that target has wandered far away and someone else is clearly closer.
Four targeting personalities, assigned per enemy
Confused — mindless things (slimes, a green novice) just retaliate against whoever hit them last.
Intelligent — every archer and caster now focuses whoever's dealt it the most damage.
Lock-in — guards and warlords commit to one target until it dies, full stop.
Closest — everyone else presses the nearest real threat, the fixed version of the old default.
Goblin Leader hits harder and shrugs off more
The Deep Woods boss is tougher across the board.
HP 160 → 200
Weapon dmg 12 → 13
New: Resist +3 flat, +10%
Slime Princess & enraged Goblin Leader kits reworked
Both bosses' summons and finishers now arrive on a fixed schedule under the new cooldown system, instead of the old "pull a random card if you've gone a while without one" logic.
New

Monster Swarms, the Market, and Fenwatch

Monster Swarms — a wave-defense portal on three maps
A swarm portal opens periodically on the Drowned Shrine, Warren Throne, and (new) Forsaken Sands maps. Step in and fight through escalating waves, one every few turns — survive them all for a large gold and XP bonus. The portal then closes for a while before it can be triggered again.

Player Market & direct trading
List gear for other players to buy, or trade directly with someone in your party. The market takes a 5% listing fee up front (whether it sells or not) and an 8% cut on completed sales — both gold sinks, neither paid to another player.
Two new reptiles prowl the desert
The Reptile Monk and Reptile Mage join the Soldier and Spearman, filling out the swarm's escalating waves and the wider Forsaken Sands roster.
Fenwatch, a new hub, and a reshuffled world map
Maps were reflowed around Fenwatch, a new town between Millhaven and the desert. The world map now scrolls, and every area you've visited shows a suggested level right on its tile.
Season 1 level cap: 35
Characters cap out at level 35 for now. XP earned past the cap simply isn't wasted chasing a bar that won't move — it's discarded instead of piling up uselessly.
Balance

Swordsman down, caster kit up

Swordsman's finisher toned down
Coup de Grâce was hitting too hard for a 1-AP card that dumps your whole turn into it.
Weapon% 60% → 40%
HP growth/lvl 12% → 11%
Caster and Enchanter pass
Weaken debuffs hit harder, barrier spells now scale with your Max HP instead of a flat number (so they keep pace as you level), and Orb weapons trade raw weapon damage for more Intelligence. Two new cards join the caster kit: Cursed Sparkles and Step While Studying.
Weaken magnitude 5 → 8
Barrier scaling flat → Max HP %
UI & Quality of Life

Seeing your party, seeing the map

Always-on party HUD
Your party now shows up on-screen at all times during normal play — who's with you and whether they're mid-fight — without opening the full online panel.
World map: suggested levels & scrolling
Visited tiles on the world map now show a suggested level, and the map itself scrolls to fit the larger world now that Fenwatch is on it.
Another UI scale & clarity pass
A further round of resizing and layout fixes across menus, combat, and the world map for better readability.
A little more breathing room on turn timers
The server's fallback preparation and turn timeouts (the safety net for a connection that drops mid-fight) were nudged up slightly, from 20s/25s to 25s/35s.
